# log/CS005.md — Two enemies, a second lattice, and the first mutated contract field

Narrative build log for CS005. `STATUS.md` carries the current changeset's
one-line ledger; the reasoning for this closed one lives here.

CS004 poured three enemies into CS003's contract **as it shipped** and the
measure of it was how little the shared machinery changed. CS005 is the opposite
shape: the two enemies left are the two whose *readability is the feature*, and
between them they needed a second set of legal lane positions, a second
silhouette per entity, and a field that means one thing at rest and another for
0.30 seconds. The Classic roster is complete at the end of it, and so is GDD
§6.2's variant table.

⛔ **`log/CS003.md`'s changeset numbers predate the +1 renumber, and a closed log
is never revised.** The same is now true of this file. **Note, do not rewrite.**

Five phases.

---

## P1 — the boundary lattice

⛔ **No entity, no rendering, no new `C` key.** That is the whole reason the
changeset held together as one: a phase of pure geometry, settled and pinned
before anything stood on it, is what let P2 and P3 each be *one entity* rather
than an entity plus an argument about lane arithmetic.

`laneHop(well, lane, delta, dir, lo, hi)` gained optional fold bounds, defaulting
to `0` and `lanes - 1`. ⛔ **The four-argument form is pinned bit-identical to
the pre-change build** by a 16,856-case sweep — 16 wells × quarter-lane values
two lanes past each end × seven deltas × both directions — hashed over raw
float64 bytes, with `GOLDEN_SWEEP` recorded from `74fb50c`. A refactor that
changes the Vaulter's arithmetic by one ulp turns `test-cs005-p1.js` red.

`laneBoundaryLo()` / `laneBoundaryHi()` return the lattice ends. ⚠
**`laneBoundaryHi` is `lanes - 0.5` on a CLOSED well, not `lanes - 1.5`.** The
phase prompt specified the open-well answer only, and the closed value is
*forced* rather than chosen: the spec requires `boundaryFrom(Ring, 0, -1)` to
give `15.5`, and P5 asserts a settled rider's lane lies inside
`[laneBoundaryLo, laneBoundaryHi]`. A closed well has no walls, so all `lanes`
boundaries are legal; `polyAt` resolves `lanes - 0.5` to vertex 0, the long way
round the seam.

`boundaryFrom()` is the birth half-step and ⛔ **does not go through `laneHop`**
— folding an off-lattice start about the lattice bounds overshoots, and
`laneHop(Vee, 0, -0.5, -1, 0.5, 11.5)` returns lane `1.5`, a lane and a half in
one cross time, which a soak reads as a teleport. One reversal always suffices,
proven by exhaustion over every integer lane of every well in both directions
rather than asserted in a comment; each open well reverses on exactly two of its
births. Reasons in full at `RATIONALE.md#boundary-lattice`.

**Mutation-checked, six:** either default bound moved, `boundaryFrom` never
reversing, reversing twice, `laneBoundaryLo` at `0`, and `laneBoundaryHi` losing
its closed branch — each turns `test-cs005-p1.js` red.

## P2 — the Drifter, its armour, and the `killDepth` correction

`class Drifter`, `drawDrifter` plus two polys, the `drifter` kind, debug key `5`,
eight `C` keys. Registry `enemies` 4 → 5, `enemyKinds` 5 → 6.

⛔ **`killDepth` is `1 - C.RIM_CONTACT_DEPTH`, and the two shipped comments that
predicted `0` are corrected.** The call was made in-phase against those comments
and is written up in `DECISIONS.md` under its own date, because it is exactly
the kind of thing a future session rediscovers in GDD §6.1's "kills by contact,
any depth" and wants to "fix" back. The short form: `collideSkimmer()` has **no
term for where the Skimmer is**, so a `killDepth` of `0` is not "lethal on
contact at any depth", it is *lethal from the throat on the spawn step*.

**The stale `killDepth = 0` prediction was in FIVE places, not the two the
prompt named.** The prompt named `07-enemies.js`'s base class and
`09-collision.js`'s `collideSkimmer` header. GDD §4.5's "conditions 2, 3 and 5
are still unwired" paragraph, GDD §6.5's `killDepth` field row and `STATUS.md`'s
own carried-task list said the same thing. All five corrected. ⚠ **A wrong value
repeated across a doc set is worse than one wrong comment, because the second
reader finds *corroboration*.**

**The birth model.** Born at the lane **centre** it was handed; the first
`update()` half-crosses onto the lattice via `boundaryFrom()`. ⛔ The constructor
neither snaps nor takes a `well` — `spawnEnemy()` is a function of
`(kind, lane, depth)` and three closed changesets' test files assert the
constructed values. It is also the better read: a Drifter emerges from the
throat **visibly vulnerable** and only arms once it settles, at the depth where
the player has the most time. Depth climbs in **both** phases, which is why an
armoured Drifter cannot become the Thorn stall. Crosses go through `laneHop`
with P1's bounds and write `dir` back.

⚠ **One judgment call, and it is a deliberate difference from the Vaulter.** A
homing answer of `0` falls back to the stored heading rather than skipping the
beat. A Vaulter that declines a hop is merely still; a Drifter that declines a
cross stays **armoured**, and the armour budget is `DRIFT_RIDE_TIME`, not "until
the player moves". Homing changes *where* it goes, never *whether*. GDD §6.1
carries it.

⚠ **A bench-spawned Drifter rides BETWEEN lattice points forever, and that is
correct.** Debug key `5` spawns in the Skimmer's **continuous** lane, and
`boundaryFrom()` carries a fractional lane through unchanged (its own header
says so). The result stays legal and stays inside
`[laneBoundaryLo, laneBoundaryHi]` — asserted — it is simply never *on* the
lattice. Nothing the spawner or `splitLanes()` produces is fractional, so it is
a bench artefact only. ⛔ Do not "fix" it by snapping in the constructor.

⚠ **`23-main.js`'s bench header said "the bench has five and the roster has
six".** True while CS004 was the present tense and stale the moment P2 landed a
sixth key. Rewritten as the *rule* instead of the count: one key per §6.1 roster
row, none for a Carrier variant.

⚠ **The Drifter's `well.closed` freedom is asserted against the BUILT file's
text**, because there is no behavioural way to prove an absence.
`test-cs005-p2.js` slices `class Drifter` out of the concatenated script and
drops line comments before searching — ⛔ the class's own header *explains* that
`boundaryFrom` is where the topology is read, so a raw text search finds the
words in prose. Do not grow that filter into a general comment stripper.

**Mutation-checked, ten:** `killDepth = 0`, `onShot` consuming while riding,
`laneHop` with the default fold bounds, the `dir` write-back dropped, a snapping
constructor, one poly drawn always closed, the climb confined to the cross,
`boundaryFrom` replaced by `laneHop`, homing removed, and `crossDur()` not
derived — each turns `test-cs005-p2.js` red.

## P3 — the Surger, its fuse, and a mutated `killDepth`

`class Surger`, `SURGER_POLY`, `drawSurger` + `drawSurgeLane`, the `surger`
kind, debug key `6`, five `C` keys. Registry `enemies` 5 → 6, `enemyKinds`
6 → 7. ⛔ **The Classic roster is complete.**

⛔ **The discharge is `killDepth` MUTATED TO `0` AND RESTORED — no eighth
contract field, and `collideSkimmer()` grew no branch.** With `0` the depth test
is unconditionally true and the only term left is `laneHit()`, which is GDD §4.5
item 3 verbatim. `setPhase()` is the one writer of both `phase` and `killDepth`.
⚠ The same number is wrong on the Drifter and right here: there it would be
permanent, here it is a 0.30 s window behind a 0.45 s fuse. A permanent kill
zone is not a discharge.

⛔ **THE PROMPT NAMED THE MUTATION AND NOT ITS RESTORE, AND THE RESTORE IS THE
HALF THAT FAILS SILENTLY.** A discharge that ended without putting the rim band
back leaves a permanently lane-lethal enemy, indistinguishable downstream from a
bug in `collideSkimmer` and reachable only after ~3 s of play. Hence one writer.

⛔ **THE STALE `killDepth` PREDICTIONS WERE IN TWO MORE PLACES — the two P2 had
just rewritten.** `07-enemies.js`'s base class said "NOTHING IN THE ROSTER IS
ZERO" and `09-collision.js`'s `collideSkimmer` header said a zero here is an
unaccountable death. Both were true when written and both are now half-true: a
**resting** zero is still wrong, a **transient** one behind a fuse is what item
3 is. Both corrected, and both now say which kind they mean. ⚠ P2 found the same
shape one changeset earlier — **a comment that names a value rots the moment a
second entity means something else by it.**

⚠ **THE PROMPT'S CYCLE TABLE DECIDED SOMETHING THE CONSTANT TABLE DID NOT.** It
attributes "depth rises at `C.SURGE_CLIMB`" to the `climb` line only, and unlike
the Drifter's spec it carries no "in both phases" ⛔ — so the climb is the climb
phase's, and shipped that way. ⚠ Which makes `PLANNED-FEATURES-CS005.md`'s
"throat→rim ≈ 6.7 s" (`1 / SURGE_CLIMB`) **not** the shipped figure: the climb
owns 2.60 of every 3.35 s cycle, so it is ≈ 8.6 s. `00-config.js` and GDD §6.1
carry the honest number and the reason. The pause was kept because it is a
fourth channel on the fuse; ⛔ if CS006 wants 6.7 s it raises `SURGE_CLIMB`, it
does not move the climb into the other two phases.

⚠ **THE SURGER KILLS BY TWO OF §4.5's FIVE CONDITIONS**, the only entity that
does: its resting `killDepth` is the rim band, so item 1 applies to it exactly
as to a Vaulter, on top of item 3. Free, correct, and in none of the planning
docs; §4.5, §6.1 and the class now say it.

⚠ **THE FUSE AND THE DISCHARGE ARE ONE DRAWER AND THE WIDTH IS THE ONLY
DIFFERENCE**, because the fuse reaching the rim and the lane going live are the
**same instant**. ⛔ `SURGE_LIT_WIDTH` is the discharge's only: if the fuse
proves too faint in traffic the fix is a second multiplier, since raising this
one costs the state change its whole read.

⛔ **The lane is never lethal during the telegraph**, checked through the real
`G.update()` rather than by inspection. ⛔ `C.SURGE_DISCHARGE <
C.RESPAWN_INVULN` is asserted from the constants: §4.4's push only *lowers* a
depth, so it does nothing against a zero.

**Mutation-checked, ten:** a lethal telegraph, an unrestored `killDepth`, the
timer not reset at a transition, the climb running in every phase, a Surger born
armed, a written `lane`, `shotAlpha()` on the fuse, the live lane losing its
width multiplier, the fuse borrowing the Thorn's scratch, and `SURGE_DISCHARGE`
raised past `RESPAWN_INVULN` — each turns `test-cs005-p3.js` red.

### ⛔ The finding: on a boundary rider, the LATTICE is where §17 item 3 stands

The Drifter's bound was mutation-checked the way CS003 P5's was proved:
`Drifter.startCross()` changed to **wrap instead of fold**, so a cross on the
13-lane Vee runs from lane `0.5` to lane `12.5`. The result was **not** what the
phase prompt predicted, and the difference is worth the paragraph.

| Assertion | Verdict under the wrap mutation |
|---|---|
| Range check, `lane ∈ [0, lanes-1]` | ⚠ **green** — `laneNormalize` clamps the in-flight lane back inside |
| Per-tick lane SPEED bound | ⚠ **green** — see below |
| ⛔ Settled Drifter is on the lattice | **RED** — settles at lane 12, a lane *centre*, outside `[0.5, 11.5]` |
| ⛔ Every cross spans one lane, or half | **RED** — `crossDelta` of 12 |

⛔ **The speed bound stayed green, and that is a property of the entity rather
than of the test.** `crossDur()` is `DRIFT_CROSS_TIME * |crossDelta|`, so a
cross of *any* length still travels at one lane per `DRIFT_CROSS_TIME` — a
wrapped cross is not faster, only twelve times longer. The Vaulter's hop is
caught by a speed bound only because `VAULT_HOP_TIME` is **fixed**. So on this
entity the lattice assertion is not a nicety layered on top of the soak; it is
the only thing standing where §17 item 3 stands for the Vaulter. Recorded in
GDD §3.5 and `RATIONALE.md#boundary-lattice`.

The complementary mutation confirms the bound is not dead code: `crossDur()`
shortened tenfold turns the **speed** bound red on all six wells while the
lattice assertions stay green. The two catch disjoint failures.

### ⚠ Three things the six-kind board changed, all measured

1. ⚠ **A per-well bolt and a per-well split are no longer per-well properties.**
   `test-cs004-p5.js` asserts both per well and passes on a list of three, where
   a Weaver is one draw in three. On six it is one in six: 5,000 ticks produced
   no bolt on the Flat and no split on the Double-Vee. Neither is a property of
   a well shape — a bolt needs a Weaver to survive to its apex hold, a split
   needs the player to actually shoot a Carrier — so both were aggregated across
   the six wells, exactly as CS004 aggregated the Thorn chip for the same
   reason. The per-well claim that carries the lane assertions is "all six
   roster **classes** were here", and that holds on every well.

2. ⚠ **The "wall was exercised" guard could not see the new entity.** A Drifter
   settles on half-integers and can *never* stand in lane `0` or lane
   `lanes-1`, so the CS003/CS004 form of the guard is blind to the one entity
   whose wall behaviour is new. Widened to "an enemy reached an end lane, **or**
   a Drifter settled on an end boundary".

3. ⛔ **A RIM-PARKED CARRIER IS A FIXED POINT, and it stalled a seeded run.**
   On the recorded input list alone, seed 21936494 ran the full 30,000-tick cap
   without reaching the stop; the board at every sample was **one Carrier at
   lane 7, depth 1.00, with the quota spent**. Not a hang and not the Thorn
   stall — a Carrier is "slow, one lane, never hops" (GDD §6.1) and has **no rim
   behaviour at all**, so a well whose only survivor is a rim Carrier never
   clears, and a scripted player whose rotation never reaches lane 7 neither
   kills it nor is killed by it. ⚠ **Three of the six roster classes park rather
   than hunt**: the Vaulter hunts at the rim and the Drifter keeps crossing and
   homes, so either will find a stationary player; a Carrier, a Weaver and a
   Surger will not come to you. Fixed in the fixture, not the build — the
   seeded runs now drive `replay` **plus a wall-to-wall pin**, which is what a
   real player is and a fixed rotation pattern is not, and is still a pure
   function of the tick index so a failing seed stays replayable. ⛔ Carried
   into CS006's `STATUS.md`: it is the same design call as the Thorn stall seen
   from the other side.

### ⚠ "birth" is the pre-update state and nothing else

The soak is where that became observable. A Drifter appended by the spawner or
by a split lands *after* the entity pass has walked past it, so it is inspected
once with the constructor's values still on it — `phase === "birth"`,
`crossDelta === 0`. Its first `update()` calls `startBirth()`, which arms the
half-cross through `beginCrossTo()` and leaves the phase at `"cross"`. So the
birth half-cross **runs in `"cross"`** with a span of 0.5, and no Drifter is ever
seen in `"birth"` twice. The assertion available in that one frame is the birth
model itself: unarmed, and at a lane **centre**.

range and speed green), `crossDur()` shortened tenfold (speed red, lattice

mutates `killDepth` (the live-board `killDepth` check red).
