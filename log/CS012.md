**What shipped.** `buildDriveTrack()` and `MUSIC_TRACKS.drive` in

**The composition (O13: 138 BPM, 36 bars A→B→C, untiered, unmarked).** GDD
§11.7 asks for the flagship, driving. `pulse` is sparse and tonal in E minor, so
`drive` goes the other way: A minor, four on the floor, and a pumping bass, in
the same 12 + 12 + 12 form `pulse` uses, so the lab's section marks and Paul's
reading of the two tracks line up. Six layers, each written to stand alone:
- `lead` carries the tune, and nothing else does. A 2-bar hook (A A E . D C . D)
  over Am F C G is stated twice in A, answered over Dm Am F G, climbs an octave
  through B, and is stated an octave up again at the top of C. At bar 32 it
  returns in its opening register, and an E major bar (G♯ as the leading tone)
  pulls back to bar 0, as `pulse`'s B major bar does. A detuned saw with a
  closing low-pass. GATE 3.
- `arp` is the riff: three chord tones struck 3 + 3 + 2 twice a bar, one tone
  order per section (up-down in A, falling in B, rising with an octave leap on
  the last strike in C).
- `bass` gives each section its own figure: octave eighths (A), a gallop (B),
  sixteenths (C). A resonant saw whose filter closes in 0.1 s. That figure is
  also what the test reads to find the three sections.
- `kick` plays four on the floor, a sine dropping two octaves. It is the one
  `beat: true` layer, so the rim pulses on every beat.
- `snare` hits two and four, with a ghost note in B and C, and a fill in each
  section's last bar.
- `hat` plays offbeat eighths from bar 4, filling in to every non-kick sixteenth
  in C.

Struck on every layer: `atk` ≤ 0.003, and every `rel` covers its GATE at 138
(0.5625 on `lead`, 0.1875 on the rest). A longer `rel` sounds the same, since the
engine clamps it to the note. It also moves music-lab's slowest ladder step to
76 BPM, so Paul can try tempos well below 138. One fill hit moved from step 8 to
step 9 after the first count: on the kick, the arp and the lead it made the worst
step 15 nodes. After the move, the worst is 13.

⚠ **The gains are the composer's, not rendered and not heard.** The CS010 render
rig (chrome-headless-shell) is not on this machine, and no mix was measured.
They follow `pulse`'s shipped balance, which Paul approved: the struck rhythm
layers loud (kick 0.450, bass and snare 0.300, hat 0.250) and the tonal layers
low (lead 0.070, arp 0.060). Paul's lab session replaces them.

### O6, and why the cooldown counting from landing is the whole design

O6's table was the phase's one real arithmetic. The three candidate cooldown
origins give 64.3 % / **39.1 %** / 36.0 % of the cycle airborne, against a
Surger cycle of 2.78–3.08 s. Counting from landing is what makes §14.2's
"clearing one leaves you grounded for the next" literally true: 2.30 s of cycle
against a Surger's 2.78 s worst case leaves the player grounded for the shorter
one and airborne for none of two. Counting from takeoff would let a player be
airborne for 64 % of the time and dodge two discharges in a row, which is the
trivialisation §14.2 names. The implementation is one line — `j.cool = 0` at the
landing, not at the takeoff — and it is the line the mutation record below
checks.

`JUMP_RECOVERY` is expressed as **the cooldown's first beat**, not as a fourth
timer: `recover` ends when `cool` passes `C.JUMP_RECOVERY`, so the two can never
disagree and there is nothing to keep in step. That also makes the lethal window
exactly the first 0.20 s of the 1.40 s, which is what O6 says.

### Why the immunity is a `return` and not a `killDepth`

The alternative the codebase had been predicting for three changesets was to
give the Skimmer a `depth` and let `collideSkimmer()` compare two depths — four
shipped comments said so in as many words (`07-enemies.js`'s base class and the
Drifter's constructor, `09-collision.js`'s header, `02-state.js`'s dive note),
and GDD §4.5 and §6.5 said it twice more. Every one of them is corrected in this
commit, because the skip is strictly better on three counts:

1. **One line covers the whole roster.** Seven contact killers, three of which
   (armoured Drifter, Weaver bolt, Surger discharging below the rim) the rim
   sweep deliberately does not cover, are all handled by the same `return`. A
   depth term would need `killDepth` to mean something new on each of them.
2. **It takes the rim sweep with it.** R3's wording — "the rim sweep never runs
   there, because fire is refused" — is not quite right as mechanism:
   `input.fire` is a level and stays true while airborne, so a fire-gated sweep
   WOULD run. Skipping the pass is what actually delivers R3's claim, and
   `test-cs012-p5.js` asserts it directly (a firing airborne craft kills nothing
   it flies over). ⛔ **That is a correction of the plan's reasoning, not of its
   answer.**
3. **The build keeps exactly one two-depth comparison**, the dive strike, which
   is the property `11-dive.js` and `09-collision.js` both lean on.

### The high-pass: three choices inside O7's one sentence

O7 fixed the group's shape (`{ hz, tc }`, `setTargetAtTime`, never `.value`) and
left three things to the build.

- **Where in the chain.** "After the sweep and before the limiter" is O7's, and
  the reason is worth writing down: the duck and the dip sit AFTER the limiter
  because CS010 MEASURED a 0.5 duck coming out at −1.9 dB in front of one. A
  tone control is the opposite case — it should be inside the limiter's view, so
  what the limiter holds is the sound the game asked for. `ensureGraph()` wires
  downstream first, so the node is built between the limiter and the sweep and
  the signal runs sweep → high-pass → limiter.
- **What "off" is.** A bypass switch would mean connecting and disconnecting a
  node, which cannot be ramped. "Off" is instead a resting **0 Hz** cutoff — a
  pass-through — so the node stays in the path permanently and both directions
  are one `setTargetAtTime`. `AUDIO_HP_OFF_HZ` names it once.
- **Q.** Fixed at the Butterworth `-3.0103` dB and ⛔ **not a tunable**, which is
  the decision that keeps `test-cs009-p5.js` unedited: a flat high-pass is at or
  under unity at every frequency, so D16's headroom model needs no term for it.
  A peaking high-pass would be a different effect rather than a louder one, and
  a host that wanted one would be asking for a filter this group does not
  describe. `test-cs012-p5.js` asserts `Q <= C.FILTER_Q` and then RUNS
  `test-cs009-p5.js` in a child process rather than copying its model.

### MEASURED — the phase-length assertion, and a float trap

⛔ **`Math.ceil(limit / C.FIXED_DT)` is NOT the number of steps a count-up timer
spends in a phase**, and the two disagree in BOTH directions. `C.FIXED_DT` is
`1/60`, which is not a binary fraction, so `t` accumulated by repeated addition
drifts: `C.JUMP_TIME` 0.90 s takes **54** steps where `ceil` says 54, and
`C.JUMP_RECOVERY` 0.20 s takes **13** where `ceil` says 12. The first draft of
this file asserted the step counts and went red on the second one. It now
asserts the property instead — *the first step at or past the limit is the last
one in the phase* — plus the duration to within one step. ⛔ **A future test that
pins a phase length by `ceil(limit / DT)` is asserting a float accident.**

### The lift's geometry

O7 says "raised outward from the well's centroid by up to `C.JUMP_LIFT` rim
radii". That is applied AFTER the projection, in screen space, and the reason is
`perspective()`: depth is capped at 1, so a point "outside the rim" expressed as
a depth above 1 would silently collapse back onto the rim and the silhouette
would lose a vertex on every well — the same trap `SKIMMER_POLY`'s header warns
about for its own `d` offsets. Each projected point is pushed along its own
outward direction from `wellCentroid()` mapped to screen space, by
`lift × C.WELL_RADIUS`, which is what makes "rim radii" a unit rather than a
figure of speech. The parabola is `4u(1 − u)`: 0 at takeoff, `C.JUMP_LIFT` at
half time, 0 at landing, so the craft touches down exactly where it left.

⛔ **The shadow is the UNLIFTED outline of the same craft**, stroked first so the
lifted one reads over it. `skimmerPoints()` returns the one shared scratch
array, so the two paths have to be built and stroked in turn — which is exactly
what `drawPoly` then `glowStroke` do, and why the shadow cannot be hoisted.

### Why the view carries `mode` when `combo > 1` would do

Plan §6 lists three view fields, `combo`, `comboFill` and `mode`, and
`hudComboAlpha()` reads both `mode` and `combo`. `combo > 1` alone would already

and "absent at ×1" — and a reader who deletes one of them should have to notice

### O3's lapse, read literally

O3: "+0.5 every `C.COMBO_KILLS_PER_STEP` kills. A window with no kill costs 0.5,
and each further window costs another 0.5, down to ×1. **A death is ×1 at once,
with the kill count emptied.** The window restarts on every kill."

⛔ **The kill count is emptied on a DEATH and on a step up, and NOT on a lapse** —
O3 names it for the death alone, and this phase took that literally rather than
tidying it. So a player who stops firing for three seconds loses a half step but
keeps their progress toward the next one. `test-cs012-p4.js` asserts it as its
own line, so a later session that "fixes" it has to argue with the plan rather
than with a silence.

`since` wraps at the window (`since -= C.COMBO_WINDOW` in a `while`) rather than
being a pure time-since-last-kill, and that is what makes the HUD ring refill and
empty again for each further half step — the loss the player is being shown. The
`while` never runs twice at `C.FIXED_DT`, but it is a `while` so a larger `dt`
cannot silently skip a step.

### Item 8 in Overdrive is asserted PER CALL, not per step

`test-cs008-p2.js` compares a step's score delta against the step's events summed
at ×1. That form does not survive a multiplier, because **a step can span a step
up**: four kills in one Purge with three banked means the first three pay at ×m
and the fourth at ×m + 0.5, and "that step's multiplier" is then not a number.
Reconstructing the pairing would mean reconstructing the order the Purge, the
shot pass and the rim sweep resolve in — a re-implementation of the thing under
test.

So the soak spies `addScore` and records `state.combo.mult` live at every call,
and classifies each call by whether `state.tally.kills` has just risen (the kill
sites increment it immediately before they score). Each **kill** call must equal
a GDD §7 price off the board times the multiplier recorded at that call, and the
multiset of board prices must be empty at the step's end; each **other** call
must equal an unmultiplied literal — the chip's 5, or one of the clear bonuses.
That is order-free, stronger than a per-step total, and it is what makes
"multiplying a chip is red" a direct consequence rather than a hope.

⚠ **One fixture subtlety.** The board's price multiset holds prices **> 0** only.
A zero-price death is either a kill site paying `e.points()` of 0 (a Thorn taken
by its last chip, whose worth is its chips) or no kill site at all (a Weaver
bolt self-terminating at depth 1), and the two are the same number; a kill call
of 0 is accepted as itself. The first draft matched zeros against the board and
went red on a bolt at tick 6341.

⚠ **And one that cost a full run.** `gddPoints()` began as a copy of
`test-cs008-p2.js`'s, closed over the outer build's classes. This file builds
**seven** games (the base, five mutants and the Classic pair), and `instanceof`
is per build: every price came back 0 and every non-vacuity check passed on an
empty count. It now takes the build as its first argument.

### Classic, and what "bit-identical" is asserted against

Six mutation strings take the combo out of the build: one per kill line, one for
`comboDeath()` in `killSkimmer()`, one for `updateCombo()` in the play step.
The three `addScore(e.points() * comboMult()); comboKill(state); sfx(...)` lines
are textually identical, so each string carries the line that follows it
(`break;`, `continue;`, or the Purge's own `if`) — and because `buildGame`
refuses a string that is not in the build exactly once, the list is its own
uniqueness proof. A 6,000-step Classic session hashes identically, step for
step, with all six applied, and `state.combo` comes out of it at its shipped
default. A 3,000-step **Overdrive** session spends the same draws and holds the
same board with the same six applied, which is the other half: the multiplier
feeds back nothing but the score.
