`src/21-telemetry.js` was a one-line placeholder. It is now twenty-nine columns,

### ⛔ No baseline moved, and that is the phase's whole claim

The prompt's standing order was that a moved determinism hash would be a bug
rather than a re-record. **None moved.** `P1_DETERMINISM_HASH` is untouched at
`3661952239`, `GOLDEN_LANES` is still on its original `9ebd27b` recording, and
the three closing soaks are green with no edit. CS007's re-record budget stays

The headline assertion is stronger than "the suite still passes": **10,000 ticks

separate builds of the same seed, with the capture-on run taking 325 rows and
reaching past level 1 so the comparison is not vacuous. An instrument that
perturbs the run it measures is worse than no instrument, and this is the form
that says so rather than the form that hopes so.

### The columns — twenty-nine, and three of the phase prompt's calls were mine

`PLANNED-FEATURES-CS007.md` §6.3's list is ⚠ PREDICTED and the prompt handed the
final one to this phase. Three deviations, each deliberate:

⛔ **EIGHT heat-derived columns, not the six the prompt enumerated.** The rule it
states is *"sample every heat-derived value"*; the list under it names six and
`CLAUDE.md` names **seven accessors** plus `heat()` itself. `vaultInterval()` and
`vaultRimInterval()` are two of the seven, and a log missing two rows of the
curve cannot be replotted against a retune of them — which is the one job this
file has. The rule won over its own enumeration.

⛔ **`maxCombo` ships as a fourth known-constant column** beside `score`, `mode`
and `startDepth`. The prompt named three; the ⚠ cross-check against the Worker's
seven registered `statsFields` names `max_combo`, and the argument for shipping a
column at a known constant — *a column added later invalidates every log recorded
before it* — applies to it exactly as it applies to `score`. With it the mapping
onto the Worker's seven is **total**: `level_reached`, `mode`, `start_depth`,
`wells_cleared`, `purges_spent`, `max_combo`, `deaths` all have a column, and the
two instruments cannot disagree about what a run was. All four live in
`C.TELEMETRY_PLACEHOLDER`, and ⛔ each key is *deleted* from that object by the
changeset that gives its column a real source.

⛔ **NO PER-KIND KILL COLUMNS**, which §6.3 proposed as *"`shotsFired`, `kills`
per kind"*. Nine kind columns today, twelve after GDD §6.4's three Overdrive
enemies: a column per kind **guarantees** the column list churns, which is
exactly what GDD §15.6's edit-them-together rule exists to prevent. `kills` is
one number — enemies the player destroyed, by shot or by Purge. The question
§6.3 attached to it (*"which enemy is actually killing the run"*) is answered by
`deaths` and `thornDeaths`, which are about deaths and not about kills.

⚠ **`divesCompleted`, not `divesSurvived`.** A diver who loses a life to a Thorn
respawns and finishes the dive, so "survived" would have been a column named for
something it does not count — GDD §15.6's own trap, in the file that names it.

**Two sawtooth columns exist because two were already on `state`**:
`spawnRemaining` and `purgeUses`, both re-armed by `enterWell()`. That matters
more than it looks: without them the three-kind classification would have been
theory, and the ⛔ "sawtooth columns are excluded **by name** from any
monotonicity check" would have had nothing to exclude. The test asserts every
cumulative column never falls across a run **and** that every sawtooth column is
*observed to fall*, so the exclusion is load-bearing rather than decorative.

### ⛔ The counters are on `state`, not inside `Telemetry`

Eight cumulative counters live in `state.tally` (`02-state.js`), incremented at
the one place each event happens, and read by exactly one consumer. Three
reasons, in order of weight:

1. ⛔ **They are maintained whether or not capture is on.** Capture is a session
   switch a player flips mid-run; counters that only started counting at the flip
   would make every `deaths` and `wellsCleared` in the log a lie about the run.
2. `newState()` resets them, so a run's totals belong to the run and
   `startGame()` needs no second reset path to drift from.
3. CS008's HUD and the leaderboard's registered stats read the same numbers
   without going near telemetry.

⛔ **Nothing in the simulation branches on one.** That is the property the
capture-ON/OFF hash proves, and it is written at the field's definition as the
rule a future reader has to keep.

**One field, not eight**, so `test-registry.js`'s state inventory moves by one
and a ninth counter costs that file nothing. `test-cs002-p1.js`'s sum guard is
untouched and still green.

**Nine increment sites, all one-liners, all write-only:** `shotsFired`
(06-shots, inside the fire branch so refused presses do not count), `kills`
(09-collision, read off `e.dead` after `onShot` rather than off its return value
— *was the shot consumed* is a different question from *did the enemy die* — plus
both Purge branches), `deaths` (09-collision, below the invulnerability guard so
a declined kill is not a death), `purgesSpent` (uses 1 and 2 only; a player
mashing an empty button spends no charge), `thornDeaths` and `divesCompleted`
(11-dive), `wellsCleared` (23-main, at the `wellCleared()` edge rather than
inside `startDive()`, which the suite also drives directly), and
`spawnBlockedTicks` at **both** of the spawner's refusals.

⛔ **`deaths` is counted, never derived as `START_LIVES - lives`.** CS008's
extra-life awards raise `lives` mid-run, and the derived form would start quietly
under-reporting the day they land.

⚠ **`spawnBlockedTicks` counts two different causes in one column** — the release
budget (threats, CS007 P1) and `C.ENEMY_CAP` (entities). They are told apart in
the file by reading `thornsStanding` beside it, which is the column P1's split
exists to make visible. A well full of Thorns blocks at the cap and not at the
budget, and that is the shape a reader is looking for.

### ⛔ A closed test was rewritten in place, and it is `test-cs007-p2.js`

`heat()` had exactly one call site in the build — `heatT()` — and P2 asserts it
by scanning the stripped build: *"no call site computes heat inline, so there is
exactly one route from the clock to a value"*. The `heat` column is a second
caller, and the prompt ⛔ mandates it.

**The claim is unchanged and the count is still exact.** What moved is that the
build gained a second *kind* of reader, one that derives nothing: `telemetryRow()`
records the curve as a column and feeds no entity, no spawner and no collision

⚠ **And the neighbouring `climbMult()` assertion did NOT need touching, for a

luck the first time and a rule the second: **every heat accessor in the row
builder is passed its level explicitly**, which is both the honest form for a row
that records the level it describes and the form that keeps P2's five-call-site
assertion meaning what it says.

### The surface, and what it must never become

⛔ **Export writes to `console.log`.** It is the only path that works on
`file://`, which the built game must open and play from by double-click. The
`navigator.clipboard.writeText` attempt sits beside it inside a try/catch **and**
catches the returned promise's rejection — `writeText` rejects asynchronously
rather than throwing, so a bare try/catch would have been an unhandled rejection
on every press outside a secure context. Absence is the normal path
(`EXTERNAL-FILES.md` rule 1). ⛔ Never an `<a download>`, never a `fetch`; the
test scans the built module for both, with comments stripped.

**Two new bench keys, `t` and `e`.** ⚠ They are the first debug keys in the build
that are safe inside a hashed run: `r`, `w` and the seven digits are on three
closed soaks' `FORBIDDEN` list because each moves the run's stream or its clock,
and neither of these touches the simulation at all. ⛔ **The soaks' lists are
therefore correct without them**, and §5 of the new test is what proves it rather
than the list.

### The sample site — one call, and where it sits is a decision

`Telemetry.sample(state)` is called from `Game.update()`, **above** the dive
branch and **below** the game-over stop. Above the dive, so the ~2.6 s a run
spends in a dive is sampled rather than being a hole in the log; below the stop,
so a stopped run stops logging. That placement is what makes it **one** call site
— `update()` has two returns below it, and a sample at the foot of the function
would have silently covered only the gameplay half. The consequence is that a row
is the simulation as a step *begins*, with `state.time` already advanced, and
that is written at the call site.

`C.TELEMETRY_INTERVAL` is 0.50 s of **simulation** time, so a slow frame, a
hit-stop freeze and a headless soak driving `update()` directly all sample
identically. ⚠ **A clock that went backwards is a new run** — `startGame()` puts
`state.time` back to 0, and a sample clock left at the old run's time would
silence the instrument for as many minutes as the previous run lasted. The rows
carry `seed`, so a restart is legible in the file rather than hidden.

### What was not built, and why it is a rule rather than a preference

⛔ **No persistence.** `kit-storage` owns the keyspace and `Profiles.keyFor(base)`
is the one route to a key; `src/22-meta.js` is a one-line placeholder and the
only `Kit*` reference in `src/` is `shell.html:38`'s leaderboard bridge. There is
no keyspace and no `Profiles`, so writing rows anywhere today would mean the game
choosing a raw `localStorage` key name — forbidden outright. The test asserts the
built module names no storage API at all, comments stripped. ⛔ **CS011 owns
persistence, the profile scope and GDD §15.6's `read()` envelope-version
rejection**, and it is wiring rather than a rewrite because the row shape and the
export already exist.

⛔ **Nothing new loads at runtime, so `EXTERNAL-FILES.md` gains no row.** Said
here explicitly because "no change" and "not considered" look identical in a
diff.

## P5 — the fifth soak, the acceptance sweep, the difficulty document, and the close

`scratchpad/test-cs007-p5.js` (94 assertions) is the fifth soak file, and it owns
**the escalating run**. **34 test files green, zero skips.**

### ⛔ Why it is a fifth file, and what makes it different from the other four

`STATUS.md` set the rule at CS005's close: *"Four soaks, and they prove different
things on different boards… A future changeset extends the pattern with a fifth
file rather than widening a closed one."* The distinguishing property here is not
which kinds are on the board — it is that **every one of the other four PINS a
level and runs one band.** `test-cs003-p5.js` is the Vaulter soak at level 2,
`test-cs004-p5.js` the three-kind band at 7, `test-cs005-p5.js` and
`test-cs006-p5.js` the full board at 23. This file is the only one that lets the
level move: twenty runs from level 1 to the game-over stop, escalating by clearing
wells.

⛔ **It therefore arms NO `C` fixture at all, and the absence IS the fixture.**
The other four raise `C.ENEMY_CONCURRENT` to `C.ENEMY_CAP` to keep a board busy;
doing that here would flatten the concurrency ladder, which is one of the things
under test. The whole of `C` is serialized before the first case and compared
after the last one — a stronger "left the build as it found it" than four
individual restores, and available only to the file that arms nothing.

### ⛔ Heat is in the hash, and the file records no baseline number

`heat(st.level)` and all seven accessors are folded into the determinism hash
every tick, so a retuned clamp moves it and a `state`-only comparison could not.
⛔ **It records no baseline constant**: the claim is self-consistency — identical
in one process, identical across two processes, different on a different seed —
which needs no recording and cannot be laundered. CS007's re-record budget was
spent at three and this phase did not spend a fourth.

⛔ **The hashed run is asserted to have crossed an introduction boundary.**
Measured: seed 20260831, 10,000 ticks, **level 6 reached, 1 restart, heat max
0.6654**, with `vaulter`, `carrierVaulter` and `weaver` all on the board. Without
that assertion, "determinism with heat in it" is a claim about a clock that is
exactly 0 for the whole window.

### ⛔ The escalation as an assertion — GDD §1.1 P3, on a level reached by playing

`test-cs007-p3.js` owns the pinned-band form: top up `spawn.remaining` so the
level cannot move, and assert each band's eligible set. That is the schedule's
arithmetic. This file asserts the same schedule as a property of a run that clears
wells — ⛔ **the minimum level at which each of the seven kinds was ever seen on a
live board is at or above its scheduled level.**

⛔ **The three Carrier variants are told apart by `cargo` and never by class.**
GDD §6.2's three rows are one class with three cargoes, so a classifier that
stopped at `instanceof Carrier` could not say which schedule row put it there —
and rows 2, 6 and 7 are exactly that distinction. `thorn` and `weaverBolt`
classify as *not rows*, because a Thorn is laid by its parent and a bolt is fired
by one.

⛔ **Each boundary run starts ONE LEVEL BELOW its row**, so both halves — absent
below, present at or after — come out of one continuous run. Measured:

| Row | Started at | First appeared at | Ticks below the line | Arrivals below it |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `vaulter` 1 | 1 | 1 | — | — |
| `carrierVaulter` 3 | 2 | **3** | 1,287 | 10 |
| `weaver` 5 | 4 | **5** | 1,116 | 14 |
| `drifter` 9 | 8 | **9** | 2,268 | 25 |

| `carrierDrifter` 18 | 17 | **18** | 2,192 | 33 |

⚠ **`carrierSurger` first appears one level LATE, at 24**, and that is the
schedule working rather than failing: the pick is uniform over a set of seven, so

⛔ **Mutation-checked.** Moving the Weaver's row from 5 to 3 in `C.SPAWN_SCHEDULE`

makes that possible.

⚠ **The non-vacuity floor is 5 arrivals and the measured minimum is 10.** Set at
the measurement it would go red on any innocuous retune, which is the opposite of
what a non-vacuity guard is for — the deliberate contrast with
