**Calls made while building, none of them design.**

- *The phase set is FOUR, not three, and `setPhase()` writes three fields.* W3
  names hover / telegraph / discharge; the shipped cycle starts at `"climb"`, so
  that one writer owns `phase`, `killDepth` **and** `aloft` together. That is the
  Surger's rule with the ninth field folded in, and it is what makes "pushed to
  0.55" a single call: the Warden reads its own depth, sees it below 1, and
  `setPhase("climb")` puts the whole contract back — shootable-but-declining,
  contactless, and armed afresh. A separate `aloft = false` somewhere else would
  be the second writer the Surger's header warns about.
- *`WARDEN_ARM` is per LIFT-OFF, not per Warden.* W3's respawn arithmetic
  ("re-climbs in ≥ 1.79 s and needs ARM + TELEGRAPH more") only reads that way if
  a pushed Warden is armed again, so `struck` is cleared by `setPhase("climb")`.
  MEASURED from the constants in the test: 2.836 s to its first discharge after a
  respawn, against a 1.5 s window.
- *A strike never begins mid-hop.* `lane` is continuous through a crossing, and
  at a half-integer **both** neighbours are exactly `HIT_LANE_TOL` away — a
  discharge started there would cover two lanes, which is not "its own lane"
  (W3). The clock HOLDS rather than restarting, the Vaulter's rule, so the strike
  lands on the step the hop does and no beat is lost.
- *`landHop()` exists for the push and not for the hop.* A Warden pushed
  mid-crossing would otherwise be parked on a half-integer permanently, which is
  the same two-lane discharge arriving by another route.
- *The beam runs DOWN, and that is the opposite of the Surger's fuse on
  purpose.* A Surger arms its lane from the throat up at you; a Warden is already
  above you and reaches down. Both say the same sentence and in both the tip
  ARRIVING is the instant the lane goes live, so the gesture transfers and the
  direction says which entity it is.
- *One unplanned constant, `C.WARDEN_BEAM_WIDTH` 2.20 ⚠.* W6 says the beam is
  "the Surger's fuse, drawn short", and that fuse's whole state change is its
  WIDTH (`SURGE_LIT_WIDTH`). Reaching for the Surger's own number would be one
  entity reading another's tunable; a bare 2.2 inline is forbidden. It ships as a
  per-entity multiplier on `laneLineWidth()`, ⚠ provisional with the rest of the
  palette, and it is in `SKIPPED-PLAYTESTS.md`'s knob list.

## P4 — the Mimic: reflect-then-open, a projectile subclass, and a cut in one row

**What the plan asked for, and what it got.** MI1–MI3, every recommendation,
built as answered. The two shapes it settled are worth writing down because
neither is obvious from the contract alone.

**MI1 — the budget IS the cycle, and it is structural rather than a counter.**
The plan's §1.7 measurement is the whole design: held fire meets a Mimic
**fifteen times a second**, so an entity that reflected every shot would fill
`C.ENEMY_CAP` in about a second. Reflect-then-open answers that without a rate
limiter, a cooldown or a per-cycle latch — reflecting is the only thing that
*opens* it, and an open Mimic reflects nothing, so "at most one reflection per
opening" is a property of the two states rather than a number anybody has to
maintain. ⚠ The build considered and did **not** take a `reflected` latch beside
the phase: it would be a second field saying what `phase` already says, and
`Weaver.fired` is only a latch because that cycle has four phases and one timer.
MEASURED on the shipped build (`test-cs013-p4.js`): under held fire in its lane

### The driver, and why it reads the board

The tenth soak's `drive(inp, i)` is a function of the step index alone. CS013's
board cannot be played that way, and ⛔ **every repair is the DRIVER, never the
build** (STATUS's rule, which grew its third clause here):

| Answer | Why the board forces it |
|---|---|
| **dodge** a `MimicShot` | it declines every shot and kills by contact (MI2), so "the deepest thing on the board" steers into the one entity a shot cannot remove |
| **collect** a hovering token | a token is taken by TOUCH and by nothing else (T4); a driver that never goes to one never collects one, and four of the five claims below are about what a collected token does |
| **jump** at anything `aloft` | killable only by the Jump and `blocksClear: true` (W4, W5) — a non-jumping driver stalls every board from L11 |
| **chip** a Thorn under Lance | a Thorn is a wall until a Lance is on and a target after (T7): `LANCE_CHIP_MULT × THORN_CHIP` is the effect's second half |

⛔ **Each is a no-op wherever the entity does not exist**, which is exactly what
lets §1's pair run this one function and hash identically: a Classic board has no
token, nothing aloft and no `MimicShot`, and `state.powers` is never written
there. That is the property that makes a board-reading driver legitimate in a
paired proof at all.

Two of the four are ⛔ **spending** rather than avoiding, and both are there
because a claim needs them: while shelled the driver stops dodging a reflection
**and** stops jumping, because a Ward is one free hit and a driver that banks it
never proves it absorbs anything. MEASURED over five `Date.now()` clocks
(7919–7963): with neither behaviour, 0–1 Ward breaks and 1–5 Lance chips; with
both, **1–4 breaks and 4–11 chips on every clock**, and every invariant zero on
every clock. ⚠ The fixture is the driver, not the seed: 7919 ships.
