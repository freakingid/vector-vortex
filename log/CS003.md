# log/CS003.md — The seeded RNG, the entity contract, the Vaulter, and death

Narrative build log for CS003. `STATUS.md` carries the current changeset's
one-line ledger; the reasoning for this closed one lives here.

⚠ **The changeset NUMBERS in this file predate CS004's split and its +1
renumber.** CS004 P1 swept the live repo — `src/`, the GDD, `DECISIONS.md`, the
tests — but this is a closed historical record and was deliberately left as
written. Read every pointer below as one lower than today's: the Dive and level
flow are **CS006**, front of house is **CS007**, onboarding is **CS014**. See
`ROADMAP.md`.

CS003 is the changeset where the build stopped being a well with a craft in it
and became a game: something climbs out of the throat, a shot can kill it, it
can kill you, and running out of lives stops the run. Five phases.

---

## P1 — the seeded RNG, the entity contract, the Vaulter

`01-rng.js` is `mulberry32` plus `rngInt` / `rngPick`. ⛔ **One source of
randomness, and it is seeded.** Nothing in `src/` may call the platform
generator — not the spawner, not a particle, not an audio variation — because
§17 item 1's determinism guarantee is only worth something if there is no
second, unseeded stream anywhere in the build. The suite scans the *built* file
for the platform generator's identifier, which is why that identifier is
deliberately not written anywhere in `src/`, not even inside a comment saying
not to use it: that comment is exactly the false positive that would train a
future session to weaken the scan.

`07-enemies.js` is the base class `Enemy` — six fields, three signatures, no
behaviour — and the Vaulter on top of it. The base exists so the ninth enemy
cannot silently forget a field; the value is the field list, not the
inheritance, and the header says so, with a ⚠ marking the slope: the first
climb rate or hop timer that lands in the base is inherited by five enemies
that do not climb, and that bug is invisible until one of them is given a
reason to read it.

The Vaulter climbs (`VAULT_CLIMB` 0.18 depth/s, ≈5.5 s throat→rim), vaults from
`VAULT_FIRST_LEVEL` (2), hunts the player at the rim ungated, and kills by
contact at `killDepth = 1 - RIM_CONTACT_DEPTH` — expressed that way rather than
as a second constant so retuning the band moves every rim-contact enemy
together. ⛔ Every hop goes through `laneHop()` **and writes back the `dir` it
returns**; that is CS001's named trap, and an enemy that keeps its own heading
grinds against an open well's end forever, one hop out and one hop back.

`14-render-entities.js` gained `entityPoints()`, the shared projection all nine
enemies will use, plus `invPerspective()` in `03-wells.js`. ⛔ **An entity's
drawn depth extent scales with its own perspective position, not by a constant
depth offset.** `perspective()` is `depth^0.55`, so a constant offset spans
~13% of the well at the throat and ~3% at the rim — an enemy drawn that way
*shrinks as it comes at you*, which is the wrong direction for the one thing
§1.1 P2 asks the player to read early.

**Judgment calls.** (a) `C.RNG_DEFAULT_SEED` was added beyond the phase's listed
constants — a default seed is a magic number and the no-inline-numbers invariant
outranks the list. (b) `entityPoints` pulls a silhouette inward when it would
reach past the rim rather than letting `invPerspective`'s clamp flatten a
rim-hunting Vaulter into a different shape. (c) the hop timer runs *during* a
hop, so hop starts are one interval apart; reaching the rim resets it, so
arrival never lunges.

**Verified:** a Vaulter that keeps its own heading fails 18 assertions across
the six open wells; a constant-depth silhouette fails the two
shrink-with-distance assertions.

## P2 — the spawner and the well lifecycle

`08-spawner.js` establishes ⛔ **the ONE way an enemy enters `state.enemies`:**
`spawnEnemy(kind, lane, depth)`. It owns §6.3's safe-spawn rule, the
`ENEMY_CAP` ceiling and the single RNG draw a spawn spends on its heading, so
CS004's Carrier split inherits all three instead of re-implementing two of them
and forgetting the third. The safe-spawn rule is enforced by **lowering** a
spawn in the Skimmer's lane to `SAFE_SPAWN_DEPTH`, never by relocating it
sideways: a Carrier split has to put its children somewhere specific ("adjacent",
"flanking"), and moving them breaks the shape the player is being taught to
read. Dropping one deeper only ever gives the player more time.

`updateSpawner` counts **up** to `SPAWN_INTERVAL` and ⛔ **holds** there when
the board is full, so a freed slot refills on the same step. A timer that reset
on a blocked beat would make a busy well quieter exactly when it should not be;
one that grew without bound would dump the backlog all at once. `wellCleared`
is ⛔ **two conditions** — quota spent AND no `blocksClear` survivor — because
"the array is empty" alone is true one tick after `startGame()` and in every gap
between spawns.

`23-main.js` gained the lifecycle: `enterWell()` / `nextWell()` /
`startGame(seed)`, the enemy pass and its end-of-frame filter, the ⚠ temporary
`WELL_CLEAR_HOLD` branch, and enemies in the z-order between the well and the
shots. Boot now calls `startGame()`; the debug cycler routes through
`enterWell()` rather than swapping `wellIndex` raw, which in CS002 was harmless
and with enemies alive strands craft on lanes the new well does not have.

**Judgment calls.** (a) `enterWell()` mints a fresh Skimmer at lane 0 — well lane
counts differ, so carrying the craft over is not free, and lives/respawn are
P4's. (b) "near the throat" for the lane redraw reuses `C.READABILITY_DEPTH`
rather than adding a knob for the same band. (c) the spawn heading draw happens
inside `spawnEnemy`, one draw per spawn, so the stream stays aligned however a
caller got there. (d) `update()` calls `enterWell()` when it finds no Skimmer,
which keeps `reset()` a pure shipped-defaults write and still gives the suite a
live well on its first step.

**Verified:** removing the safe-spawn clamp, dropping the quota half of the
clear condition, and resetting the spawn timer on a blocked beat each turn the
suite red.

## P3 — the collision pass and the Purge

`09-collision.js` is ⛔ **the one collision pass**, called from `Game.update()`
after the entity pass and before the filters, in a fixed order: shots against
enemies, then enemies against the Skimmer, each front to back. The order is
written down because §17 item 1's replay guarantee needs the same target picked
every time, and "whichever the loop happened to reach first" is only
deterministic if it is stated.

⛔ **1-D throughout.** `laneDelta` within `HIT_LANE_TOL` plus a `depth` overlap
within `HIT_DEPTH_TOL`; no projected point anywhere. A hit test that read a
projected point would pass at the rim and fail at the throat, because
`perspective()` is not linear. No tunnelling check is needed and the arithmetic
is recorded in the module header so a future session does not add one: a shot
covers ≈0.032 depth units per step against a hit band 0.10 wide.

⛔ **One shot resolves against at most one enemy per step** — the `break` is
unconditional, so an enemy that declines the shot stops its search too.
Letting a shot walk the rest of the array in the same step would make one
trigger pull kill a whole stacked lane, which is §4.2's chip-away economy
inverted.

The Purge lives here too. `state.purgeUses` counts **up**: use 1 clears every
`purgeable` enemy, use 2 kills exactly one (highest depth, then lowest lane,
then array order — all three deterministic, because §4.3 promises the player can
predict the victim), use 3+ does nothing while the count keeps rising so a HUD
can tell *spent* from *spent twice*. `input.purge` is a **level** — all four
devices write a held boolean — so the edge is detected in the game against
`state.purgeLatched`.

**Judgment calls.** (a) The Purge consumer lives in `09-collision.js`, not
`05-skimmer.js`: `CLAUDE.md`'s code map assigned it to 05, but that map also
assigned "firing" there and firing shipped in `06-shots.js` in CS002. It is a
read-order skeleton, and the Purge is mass entity destruction over the same
array the collision pass walks. P5 corrected the map. (b) `state.purgeReady`
(P2's boolean) was **replaced** by `purgeUses`, not supplemented — a boolean
cannot express §4.3's weak second use, and P2's own carried task predicted it.
No assertion was weakened. (c) The Purge resolves **before** collision, so a
charge spent on the step an enemy arrives in your lane saves you rather than
firing one step late. (d) `enterWell()` deliberately does not clear
`purgeLatched` — it is input state, not well state, and clearing it would let a
player still holding the button spend the new charge without releasing. (e) A
second use with no legal target is still spent: the charge is consumed by the
press, not the result.

**Verified:** a conditional `break` in the shot loop, a bare `(a - b)` in place
of `laneDelta`, dropping the `killDepth === null` guard, a tie-break to the
highest lane, a `>=` that loses array order, firing the Purge on the level
instead of the edge, and a first Purge that ignores `purgeable` each turn the
suite red.

## P4 — death, lives, respawn and the game-over stop

`killSkimmer()` was filled in and is the whole death sequence: the
invulnerability guard, one life, the `purgeLatched` force, the
`screen = "gameover"` stop at zero, and `Game.hitStop(C.HIT_STOP_DEATH)`.
`state` gained `lives` and `invulnTime`; the latter counts **up** and starts
**at** `RESPAWN_INVULN`, already expired, because a fresh run must not be born
invulnerable. `23-main.js` gained `spawnSkimmer()` — the one `new Skimmer` — and
`respawnSkimmer()`, which fires on the first live step that sees `skimmer.dead`,
pushes enemies down, and mints the craft in the lane it died in.

⛔ **The respawn is not a timer started at death.** `Game.update()` does not run
during hit-stop, so anything scheduled at death sits unadvanced for the whole
1.2 s. The trigger is the state itself, which also makes the sequence correct
for a headless caller that drives `update()` directly and never freezes at all.
And ⛔ the rim push happens at the respawn, not at death: pushing at death
teleports the killing enemy away during the freeze the player is staring at, and
the freeze exists to show them what happened.

**Judgment calls.** (a) ⚠ **SETTLED — Paul, 2026-08-30.** §4.4's rim push is a
**clamp over every lane**, not a narrow band at the rim. The narrow reading
leaves a Vaulter at 0.9 climbing into the kill band well inside the
invulnerability window, which is the death the rule exists to prevent. It is
deliberately more generous than §4.4's wording alone requires; do not narrow it
back. Written into §4.4 as settled. (b) The respawn lives in `23-main.js` beside
`enterWell()`, not next to `killSkimmer()`: P3's comment said P4 would fill it
in "HERE", but it cannot be inside `killSkimmer` and it moves enemies. (c) The
invulnerability clock is the **else** branch of the respawn check, so the
respawn step is not also aged and the window is exactly `RESPAWN_INVULN`, not
one step short. (d) The guard is in `killSkimmer()` and not the collision pass,
so CS004/CS005's four remaining death conditions inherit it. (e)
`runAction("restart")` clears `hitStopLeft` — `startGame()` cannot reach it, and
a fresh run must not inherit the freeze that ended the last one. (f)
`skimmerBlinkVisible()` takes the **timer**, not `state`, so `05-skimmer.js`
still reads no game global.

**Verified:** an `invulnTime` born at 0, dropping the invulnerability guard,
dropping the rim push, making the push an assignment instead of a clamp,
dropping the purge re-latch, respawning at lane 0 instead of the lane it died
in, dropping the game-over early return, aging the invuln clock on the respawn
step too, a restart that inherits the old freeze, and removing either the
hit-stop or the `lives -= 1` each turn the suite red.

## P5 — the invariant soak, the docs, and the close

`test-cs003-p5.js` owns the two §17 invariants no build phase can own alone.

**Item 1, determinism, re-verified.** It was trivially true until this
changeset, because the build contained no randomness. The hash now includes the
enemy array — lane, depth, and the hop state where a stale heading would show
up — and is taken **every tick** rather than only at the end, so a divergence
that heals still moves it. Two traps in the fixture, both recorded in the file:
the recorded input list must never press `r`, whose named action calls
`startGame()` with no argument and therefore a *time-derived* seed; and a run
reaches the game-over stop long before 10,000 ticks, after which `update()`
returns early and the remaining ticks hash nothing new, so the run restarts
through the real `startGame()` with a seed derived from its own.

**Item 3, the wall.** 5,000 ticks on each of the six open wells, driven through
the real loop with the spawner running, the collision pass live, deaths and
respawns happening, and adversarial rotation. ⛔ **The range check alone does not
catch §3.5's bug**, and that is the finding worth carrying: a hop that wraps on
a 13-lane strip sends lane 12 to lane 0, and lane 0 is *inside* `[0, 12]` — the
enemy teleports across the well without ever leaving the legal range. The tell
is the **speed**: one hop crosses one lane over `VAULT_HOP_TIME`, so no single
tick may move an enemy further than that, and the bound is derived from the
constants rather than picked. Mutating `laneHop()` to wrap on open wells leaves
the range assertion green and turns the speed assertion red.

The soak also asserts it was not vacuous — enemies spawned, enemies died, the
Skimmer was killed, an enemy reached the rim, and an enemy reached an end lane —
because every invariant above passes on an empty board, which is exactly how a
wall-behaviour test quietly stops testing the wall. Two fixtures hold the well
open: `spawn.remaining` and `lives` are topped up every tick, since a cleared
well advances to the next shape and the stop freezes the board, and either one
ends the soak early in a well it was not aimed at.

**Judgment calls.** (a) The soak asserts an enemy *reached* an end lane, not
that one *reversed* there. The reversal is `test-cs003-p1.js`'s, driven against
one Vaulter in isolation where the whole bounce sequence is exactly observable;
a live well cannot guarantee an outward hop at a wall inside 5,000 ticks, and
tuning seeds until it does would be tuning the test to pass. P5's claim is that
the rule survives a live well. (b) The determinism run restarts on the stop
rather than hashing 6,000 frozen ticks — driving `startGame()` twenty more times
is more coverage, not less, and the restart seeds are derived from the run's own.
(c) `ENEMY_KINDS` was added to `_harness.js`'s export list so the roster can be
checked against `test-registry.js`'s `enemies` count; that count is the reason
the registry exists and had no reader until now. (d) The FNV mixer is duplicated
from `test-cs002-p1.js` rather than hoisted into `_harness.js` — hoisting would
make one hash function two phases' to keep stable, and those two cases are meant
to be able to disagree.

**Verified, 117 assertions:** 10,000 ticks of one recorded input list hash
identically twice in one process and once in a child process, and a different
seed moves the hash; a `startGame()` that ignores its seed argument turns both
identity assertions red. Twenty seeded runs to the game-over stop throw nothing,
leave no NaN in `state`, and never pass `ENEMY_CAP` or `SHOT_MAX`.

## Carried forward (not CS003's to fix)

- §12's promise that a passive player dies on level 1 within four seconds is
  **not yet delivered.** It needs spawn lanes weighted toward the player's lane;
  that is onboarding tuning and belongs to CS013.
- `VAULTER_COLOR` is a ⚠ placeholder with the same standing as `SKIMMER_COLOR`,
  and `tools/glow-lab.html` — the instrument `CLAUDE.md` names for exactly this
  decision — does not exist.
- `drawWell()`'s `laneState` parameter is still unwired. Lane occupancy lighting
  belongs with the dim band, in CS005.
- ⚠ `C.WELL_CLEAR_HOLD`, `state.clearHold` and the branch in `Game.update()` that
  reads them are TEMPORARY and are CS005's to delete when the Dive lands.
- `state.screen === "gameover"` is a stop with nothing on screen but the frozen
  board. `r` restarts. CS006 owns the screen, the submission and the real restart
  flow, and should fold the `restart` debug action into it rather than leave a
  second way in.
- A rim Vaulter hunts the Skimmer's *continuous* lane, so a player parked between
  two lane centres has it hopping across them. Lethal either way; flagged for
  CS005's tuning pass in case the jitter reads as indecision rather than menace.
- `_harness.js` exists twice — the repo root holds a tracked, stale copy that
  nothing loads. A test that reaches one directory too far gets a silently
  smaller surface.
- GDD §3.3's `throatOffset` is undefined and the Flat well (11) is geometrically
  degenerate for the same underlying reason. Design call for Paul; natural
  landing spot is CS004.
- Register `vector-vortex` in the Worker's `services/leaderboard/src/registry.js`
  before any submission is attempted, and backport `kit-input` to coinless-kit.
