# log/CS004.md — Three enemies, a projectile, and the field that says what `depth` means

Narrative build log for CS004. `STATUS.md` carries the current changeset's
one-line ledger; the reasoning for this closed one lives here.

CS003 built the entity spine and one enemy to prove it. CS004 is the changeset
that spent it: three more roster rows and a projectile poured into the contract
**as it shipped**, with the flags CS003 wrote for exactly this — `purgeable`,
`blocksClear`, `killDepth` — doing the work instead of a growing list of special
cases. The measure of the changeset is how little the shared machinery changed:
the collision pass gained nothing, `updatePurge()` gained nothing, `wellCleared()`
gained nothing. What it did gain was a **seventh contract field**, and that is
the one thing here that was not free.

Five phases.

---

## P1 — the seventh contract field, the debug bench, the pointer sweep

No enemy. Four pieces of groundwork, and the first of them was a live bug.

⛔ **`anchored`, and the bug it fixes.** `respawnSkimmer()` implements GDD §4.4's
rim push as an unconditional clamp over `state.enemies` — the only place in the
build that writes an entity's `depth` from outside that entity. For every entity
that had ever existed, `depth` is a *position* and the clamp is exactly right.
For P4's Thorn it is a *length*, so every player death would permanently shorten
every Thorn past 0.55 — a free chip nobody earned, delivered silently, in the one
place nobody would look. The fix is one boolean on the base class and one line in
the respawn.

⚠ **And it landed two lines under a SETTLED marker.** Commit `1ce0011` marked the
clamp ⚠ SETTLED with the words *"Do not narrow it back"*, and `CLAUDE.md`'s rule
for a session that finds one is unambiguous: do not fix it, do not re-litigate
it, stop and say so. The phase prompt carried Paul's explicit pre-authorisation
*and* the reason the change is orthogonal — the marker is about the **depth
band** (everything above `RESPAWN_PUSH_DEPTH` comes down, in every lane) and
`anchored` is about **which entities a depth clamp means anything for**. The
comment above the line was **extended, not replaced**, so it now says both
things. The full argument is `RATIONALE.md#thorn-depth`.

⛔ **The field is documented as a statement about what `depth` MEANS, not about
movement.** A stationary enemy whose `depth` is still a position is `false`. That
wording is the whole value of the field: "does it move" would have been answered
wrongly by the next enemy that stands still.

**The debug bench.** GDD §8.1's introduction schedule is CS006's, so nothing new
would spawn on its own for two changesets and Paul needed a way to put one on
screen. Five named actions on the existing `actionKeys` path — `1`–`4` and `0` —
never a second listener. ⛔ **Digits, not letters**, because `test-cs003-p5.js`'s
recorded input list must never press `r` (restarts on a time-derived seed) or `w`
(cycles the well). Three of the four spawn digits were no-ops at the time, which
is free: `spawnEnemy()` returns `null` for an unknown kind.

⚠ **`C.DEBUG_SPAWN_KINDS` and `pickSpawnKind()`, with one trap in them.** The
interval spawner hardcoded `spawnEnemy("vaulter", ...)`. It now asks a function.
⛔ **A one-entry list spends NO RNG draw** — `rngPick` on a single-element array
still advances the run's one stream, which would move every spawn lane in every
run including the 10,000-tick replay GDD §17 item 1 hashes, and turn the
determinism test red for a reason that takes an afternoon to find. The guard is
`if (kinds.length < 2) return kinds[0]`.

**The palette, as one set.** Six Classic enemy colours at once, all ⚠
provisional, including the two nothing reads until CS005 — because a palette
picked four now and two later is a palette with a clash in it. ⛔ The constraint
they were chosen against is recorded in `C`: an enemy colour must read against
**all seven** band colours (§3.6), because the well cycles and the enemies do
not.

**The pointer sweep, which was three times the size the inventory said.**
`PLANNED-FEATURES-CS004.md` counted twelve `CS005` pointers meaning *level flow*.
P1 found **seventeen** — three more in `test-cs003-p2.js` — and then found that
stopping there would have made the repo worse: the whole tail had shifted, so
**twenty-two** more said `CS006` meaning *front of house* and **two** said
`CS014` meaning *ship*. Sweeping only the `CS005` strings would have left
`23-main.js` saying `CS006` meaning two different changesets three functions
apart. All of it is swept.

**Judgment calls.** (a) The ⚠ comment at `respawnSkimmer()` was extended rather
than replaced, so a future reader gets both halves of the settled decision in one
place. (b) The duplicate root `_harness.js` was already gone — Paul removed it in
`7817d33` — so P1 verified its absence and the green suite instead of deleting
it, and removed the stale `STATUS.md` line that was all that remained.

## P2 — the Carrier, the split, and the cargo table

`class Carrier` is the easy half. It climbs one lane at `C.CARRIER_CLIMB` 0.11
(throat→rim ≈ 9 s, §6.1's "slow"), stops at the rim, and kills by contact at
`1 - C.RIM_CONTACT_DEPTH` — the Vaulter's expression, not a second constant, so
retuning the band moves every rim-contact entity together. ⛔ **It touches no
lane helper at all**, so "one lane, never hops" is an *absence of code* rather
than a flag, and the test asserts the strong form: exact lane equality over 3,000
ticks on all sixteen wells.

⛔ **`splitLanes(well, lane)` lives in `03-wells.js`, beside `laneHop`**, because
what makes it hard is GDD §3.5 and nothing about cargo. `laneNormalize` **clamps**
on an open well, so the naive `lane ± 1` puts both children of a lane-0 Carrier
into lanes 0 and 1 — two silhouettes in one lane, on the six wells where the
player has the least room, at the exact moment they are being asked to read a
split. That is §1.1 P2 failing, and it fails on open wells only, which is how it
would survive a closed-well playtest. The rule is **shift the pair inward, never
squash it**: a lane-0 parent on the 13-lane Vee yields children at **0 and 2**.

⚠ **The prose about that had a slip, and the concrete example is the half that
was right.** "The pair still straddles a lane, and the lane it straddles is the
parent's" cannot be true at a wall — 0 and 2 straddle lane 1. What is true, and
what keeps the trap honest, is that **one child still occupies the lane the
parent died in**.

⛔ **Both children go through `spawnEnemy()`**, and four things follow, every one
deliberate: the safe-spawn rule lowers rather than relocates (relocating would
break the shape §6.2 teaches the player to read); `C.ENEMY_CAP` applies, so a
split on a full board adds nothing and "it's only a split" is exactly the bypass
one entry point prevents; two RNG draws are spent and shift every later draw in
the run, deterministically; and ⚠ **the push happens from inside
`collideShots()`'s own loop over the array being appended to.**

That last one is safe for **three separate reasons in two files** — the loop is
index-based and re-reads `.length`, the `break` after `onShot` is unconditional
so the shot cannot walk into its own children, and removal is still the
end-of-frame `.filter()`. ⛔ The reasoning is written at the call site because a
session that does not see it will either "fix" it into a deferred queue or break
it by making that `break` conditional.

⚠ **The Purge does not split Carriers, and it works by omission.**
`updatePurge()` sets `dead` directly and never calls `onShot()`. A panic button
that doubles the enemy count is not a panic button, and an omission is exactly
what a later session unifies away — so it is a ⚠ SETTLED note at both sites.

**Judgment calls.** (a) `ENEMY_KINDS` gets **one row per Carrier VARIANT** —
`carrierVaulter`, not `carrier` — because the cargo is half of what the entity
is; that made P1's bench key `2` one string short of working, and P1's "`2`, `3`
and `4` are no-ops" assertion false, both fixed here. (b) At the rim the cargo
glyph's apex touches the hull's near tip; that is `entityPoints()`'s rim clamp
acting on both polys, it happens only in the clamp band, and fixing it would mean
changing `entityPoints`, which was out of scope.

## P3 — the Weaver, its bolt, and GDD §4.5's fourth death condition

The cycle is a phase string and one up-counting timer: climb at `C.WEAVER_CLIMB`
to `C.WEAVER_APEX` 0.55, hold `C.WEAVER_APEX_HOLD`, retreat at the faster
`C.WEAVER_RETREAT`, repeat. It should be nameable by a player watching it.

⛔ **`fired` is a per-CYCLE latch, not a cooldown**, and the bolt goes out on the
FIRST step of the hold — so the player reads a bolt already travelling while its
parent is still visibly at the apex (§1.1 P2). A cooldown would quietly turn "one
bolt per cycle" into "one per `WEAVER_APEX_HOLD`", which is 21 of them.

⛔ **The Weaver's `killDepth` is the roster's first `null`.** §4.5 item 4 is a
Weaver's *projectile*, not a Weaver, so its body never kills at any depth
**including the rim**. That is what the base class's `null` case was written for,
and it is why the collision pass needs no branch. ⚠ A Weaver sitting on top of
the Skimmer is a nuisance, and it is going to look wrong the first time.

The bolt is the mirror: `killDepth = 1 - C.RIM_CONTACT_DEPTH` (§4.5 item 4, live,
with **no new collision code**), `blocksClear = false` so a bolt in flight cannot
hold a cleared well open, `purgeable = true` because that is what a panic button
is for, and ⚠ `onShot` returns `false` — **it is not shootable**. A shootable
bolt is a free score piñata and it removes the lesson, which is that a Weaver's
output is dodged rather than answered.

⛔ **The bolt's death at `depth 1` is the step AFTER it arrives, and that is an
ordering decision.** `Game.update()` runs the entity pass, then collision, and
`collideSkimmer()` skips anything already `dead` — killing it on the step its
depth reaches 1 would make the rim step silently non-lethal. The lethal band
starts nine steps earlier, so this is belt and braces, written so the belt does
not depend on the braces.

**A finding that arrived a phase early.** `test-cs003-p5.js` compared
`Object.keys(ENEMY_KINDS).length` against `test-registry.js`'s `enemies`, and the
bolt is a kind with **no GDD §6.1 roster row** — so that equality went red the
moment the Weaver landed. STATUS had predicted it for P5. Fixed by splitting the
number in two, in the one file allowed to name a global count: `enemies` counts
roster rows and `enemyKinds` counts `ENEMY_KINDS` rows. ⛔ CS005 raises both, and
by different amounts.

**Judgment calls.** (a) A Weaver that *arrived* above the apex turns around from
where it is rather than teleporting down to the line — the bench stages one as
deep as `SAFE_SPAWN_DEPTH`, and CS006 will move the apex under live entities.
(b) `Weaver.fire()` goes through `spawnEnemy()` — the second non-spawner caller —
so a bolt inherits the cap, the safe-spawn lowering and one RNG draw; a blocked
shot is a lost beat, never a bolt held over, because the latch is set either way.
(c) `Weaver.fire()` reads the **global** `state`, the way `Carrier.onShot()`
does, because that is how `spawnEnemy()` finds the current well — any test that
drives a cycle on a well the state is not pointing at puts its bolts elsewhere.

## P4 — the Thorn, the chip economy, and the Weaver's lay

The Thorn is four flags and an `onShot`, and every one of the flags is machinery
CS003 already built: ⛔ `purgeable = false` (§4.3's "does not remove Thorns"),
⛔ `blocksClear = false` (which is *why* it is standing during the Dive rather
than an oversight in the clear check), `killDepth = null`, ⛔ `anchored = true`.
`update()` is empty. It is not an enemy in any normal sense and it lives in
`state.enemies` anyway, because everything it shares with an enemy is exactly the
machinery CS003 built and a second array would double all six wiring points to
save one boolean.

⛔ **Its `depth` is the tip of an extent rooted at the throat**, and that pulls in
two directions at once, which is the whole point. `collideShots()` does **not**
skip it — its one-line hit test is exactly right on a Thorn *because* `depth` is
the tip, so a shot stops where the Thorn starts. `respawnSkimmer()` **does** skip
it, because clamping a length is a free chip. Moving the extent to a second field
would have bought a Thorn branch in the one pass where the contract is paying for
itself.

⚠ **GDD §4.2's rapid chip-away is emergent and is not a bug.** `onShot` chips
`C.THORN_CHIP` and **consumes** the shot; the end-of-frame filter frees that
shot's `SHOT_MAX` slot the same step, so camping a thorned lane chips fast. ⛔ It
must not be rate-limited — there is nothing here to add a cooldown to, and that
is deliberate.

⛔ **`Weaver.layThorn()` only ever grows.** The cycle sends the Weaver back to the
throat every time round, so an unconditional `thorn.depth = tip` would saw the
segment down to nothing on the second climb — the Weaver eating its own work
while the player watches. `if (tip > thorn.depth)` is the rule, not defensive
programming, and it is what makes "a Weaver killed mid-climb leaves its Thorn at
the length it reached" true by construction.

⛔ **It adopts a live Thorn in its lane rather than creating a second.** Two
overlapping Thorns are two hit-point pools behind one silhouette: §1.1 P2 failing
and a scoring oddity at once. `thornInLane(state, well, lane)` is the lookup, and
it uses ⛔ `laneDelta` with `C.HIT_LANE_TOL` — the same "same lane" the collision
pass uses, so a Thorn it finds is exactly a Thorn a shot down that lane would hit.

⛔ **`drawThorn()` is the build's second draw that does not use
`entityPoints()`.** A Thorn is a segment *along* the lane, not a silhouette *at*
a point, so it reuses `drawShot()`'s shape: preallocated scratch points — and
preallocated point **pairs**, because `drawPoly(ctx, [a, b])` allocates an array
literal per call — a body from the throat to the tip, and a `THORN_TIP_LEN` crown
drawn a second time. The crown is brighter because `glowStroke` composites with
`lighter`, which is why there is no second colour and no alpha split. ⛔ Full
alpha at every depth: §10.3 governs what may be drawn *over* the throat zone, and
a Thorn is lane geometry.

**Judgment calls.** (a) The bench's `4` spawns a **zero-length, invisible**
Thorn, and that is correct — `depth 0` is the literal every bench digit uses, and
on an anchored entity it means *no length*. It is also exactly why the safe-spawn
rule can never shorten one: a Thorn is created one climb step above the throat
and grown from there. To see one, press `3` and watch a Weaver, or `0`. (b) The
lay broke three earlier *measurements* — a Weaver produces a Thorn inside the
same `update()` that spawned the Weaver — and all three were counting entities
where they should have been counting classes. (c) `thornInLane` uses
`instanceof` where the Purge and the clear check read a flag: those ask what an
entity *does*, this asks which entity a Weaver is *growing*, and inventing an
eighth contract field to answer it is the slope §6.5 warns about.

## P5 — the soak, the docs, the close

`test-cs004-p5.js` extends `test-cs003-p5.js` rather than restating it: same hash
mixer, same recorded input list, same adversarial generator, same NaN walker.
What is new is what CS004 put on the board.

⛔ **§17 item 3, and the assertion CS004 makes available.** CS003's finding was
that a range check does not catch §3.5's bug — a hop that wraps a 13-lane strip
lands inside `[0, 12]` — so it uses a per-tick lane SPEED bound. **None of
CS004's four entities hops at all**, so the available assertion is stronger and
simpler: a Carrier, Weaver, Thorn or bolt's `lane` is **exactly** the lane it
entered with, on every tick, on every well, `Object.is` and no epsilon.
`MAX_LANE_STEP` stays the Vaulter's; ⛔ it has to become **per-entity** in CS005,
when the Drifter becomes the first thing in the build that moves continuously in
lane space.

⛔ **§17 item 1, re-verified with the new draws.** A split spends two draws, a
bolt one, a laid Thorn one, and `pickSpawnKind` one per spawn on a mixed list —
all off the run's one stream, which makes the determinism claim materially bigger
than it was. The hash gained the Carrier's cargo, the Weaver's phase and latch,
and whether a Weaver is holding a Thorn: a hash blind to those would pass over a
build that had lost the split entirely. ⛔ The recorded input list is checked
**against the function** — driven into a recorder and inspected — for `r`, `w`
and the five debug digits, rather than trusted.

**§17 item 5 was untestable until this changeset.** CS003 proved both Purge uses
against a board where everything was purgeable, which cannot distinguish "reads
the flag" from "kills everything". The Thorn is the roster's first `false`, so
this is the first board on which "does not remove Thorns" means anything — and
the case also catches the Weaver laying one on the tick it was purged, which
survives too.

**§17 item 6 is a loop over the `CARGO` table**, with the kind that carries each
cargo *discovered* from `ENEMY_KINDS` rather than named — so CS005 adds two rows
and no test, and a `CARGO` row with no variant to carry it fails loudly.

⛔ **The roster count is derived, not compared.** `COUNTS.enemies` counts §6.1
roster rows, and a kind is not a roster row twice over: one kind per Carrier
variant, and the bolt is a kind with no roster entry. So the check is *the
distinct classes the kind table can build, minus the projectiles*. CS005 adds two
roster rows and four kinds and needs no edit here.

⛔ **THE FINDING OF THIS PHASE, and it is not a test problem.** The six-well soak
came back nearly empty — **three enemies born in 5,000 ticks** on the Fan.
`updateSpawner()` blocks on `state.enemies.length >= min(ENEMY_CONCURRENT,
ENEMY_CAP)`, and that counts **everything in the one array, Thorns included**.
`ENEMY_CONCURRENT` is 3. A Thorn nobody shoots is permanent, so three standing
Thorns hold the spawner shut: the quota never spends, the well never clears, and
because a Thorn does not kill, nothing threatens the player either. Measured
directly: three Thorns and 100 seconds of an untouched controller leaves the
level, the quota and the board exactly where they started.

It is unreachable in a played build **today** only because ⚠ `C.DEBUG_SPAWN_KINDS`
ships as `["vaulter"]` and no Weaver ever spawns. It goes live the moment CS006's
introduction schedule puts Weavers at L5. ⛔ P5 did not fix it — "no new
gameplay", and the fix is a real design call (does the concurrency budget count
threats or entities? does the clear condition change?). It is recorded in
`STATUS.md`, `ROADMAP.md`'s open list and GDD §6.1, named against CS006.

The soak works around it with a **documented fixture**: `C.ENEMY_CONCURRENT` is
raised to `C.ENEMY_CAP` for the six-well loop, the same class of fixture as
topping up the quota and the lives, and put back before the seeded runs. ⛔
`C.ENEMY_CAP` is untouched and still asserted — it is a readability ceiling, not
a difficulty knob.

⛔ **And one legal invariant was being violated in shipped output.**
`src/14-render-entities.js` named the homaged title in a comment — one word, from
CS002 P3 — and `src/` is concatenated into `dist/vector-vortex.html`, so it was a
string in the shipped artifact. `CLAUDE.md` and GDD §18 item 1 both say the word
appears in **no file**, comments included. Fixed here, with a ⛔ note at the site
saying why. ⚠ The design docs keep it: §18 has to name what it prohibits, and the
GDD does not ship.

**Judgment calls.** (a) The chip assertion is aggregated over the six-well soak
rather than asserted per well: splitting and laying are things a board does on
every well, but a chip needs the player standing in a thorned lane when a shot
arrives, which is a per-run accident. (b) The 20 seeded runs run at the **shipped**
`ENEMY_CONCURRENT`, because that is the game that ships; they pass because the
recorded input holds fire almost constantly, which is itself worth knowing.
(c) `test-cs004-p1.js`'s unbuilt-kind case was **deleted** rather than narrowed —
all five bench digits answer now, and a loop over an empty list is a vacuous pass.

## Carried forward (not CS004's to fix)

- ⚠ **The six enemy colours are provisional** and `tools/glow-lab.html` still
  does not exist. Whichever changeset takes the art pass owns both.
- ⚠ **`C.DEBUG_SPAWN_KINDS`, `pickSpawnKind()` and the five debug spawn actions
  are TEMPORARY.** GDD §8.1's introduction schedule replaces all of them. ⛔ The
  list is a bench, never a difficulty knob — do not tune the game by editing it.
- ⚠ **A standing Thorn holds a spawner slot** — the P5 finding above. CS006's.
- **`drawWell()`'s `laneState` parameter is still unwired.** Lane occupancy
  lighting (§3.7) belongs with the dim band, in CS006. ⛔ The Surger's telegraph
  is an entity draw and must **not** be built on `laneState`.
- **`laneHop()`'s half-lane fold point and a per-entity `MAX_LANE_STEP`** are
  both CS005's, with the Drifter as the entity that makes them matter. ⛔ Do not
  change the helper without the entity that exercises it.
- **GDD §3.3's `throatOffset` is undefined** and **the Flat well (11) is
  geometrically degenerate**. Same underlying question, both open design calls
  for Paul, neither an enemy question.
- **No scoring.** A Carrier kill, a Weaver kill and a Thorn chip all award
  nothing; `PTS_CARRIER`, `PTS_WEAVER` and `PTS_THORN` are deliberately unread
  until `addScore()` lands in CS007.
- ⚠ **`C.WELL_CLEAR_HOLD`, `state.clearHold` and the branch that reads them are
  TEMPORARY** and are CS006's to delete when the Dive lands.
